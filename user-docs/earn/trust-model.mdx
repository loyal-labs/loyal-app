title: "Technical security model"
description: "The enforcement layers and residual risks in Loyal Earn automation."
---

Earn uses several independent controls. No single control proves the complete workflow is safe.

| Layer | Enforces | Does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer authority | Setup, root configuration, manual approval, and exit | That an allowed lending action is risk-free. |
| Subscription delegation | Recurring period, per-period amount, and delegated transfer authority | That every submitted transaction matches the Smart Account policy. |
| Earn route and setup policies | The approved program interaction | That the scheduler chose the correct reserve or amount. |
| Autodeposit policy | The approved sweep path and an independent maximum per period | That the saved operating floor is enforced onchain. |
| Loyal scheduler | Operating floor and pause state; eligibility and timing | Onchain authority or transaction confirmation. |
| Market and RPC inputs | Current position and liquidity; rates and confirmation evidence | Future rates, liquidity, or protocol solvency. |

The operated Earn workflow is same-mint USDC through configured Kamino markets. The generic policy system can express other routes, so integration code must verify the active policy instead of assuming a product label is an onchain restriction.

Realtime delivery can wake a worker or client. Current authority and balances still come from Solana and the underlying protocol.

Policy limits reduce automation authority. They do not remove Smart Account, Subscriptions, Kamino, USDC, Solana, liquidity, operational, or upgrade risk.
